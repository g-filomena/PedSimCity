Night Model Issue Register

Seventeen findings in the lighting pipeline and the night behavioural layer — what each one is, the evidence for it, and the fix — followed by a sourcing pass over the twelve constants nobody had traced. Companion to Punti Luce to Lux, which documents the chain these defects sit in. Every fix here was originally a proposal for the code owner, not applied by this register — but as of 18 September, nine have since landed upstream on main itself, marked resolved where they occur, two more were addressed with a different fix than proposed here, marked revised upstream, one was considered and explicitly rejected upstream, and one (C1) that read as resolved from the diff alone turned out, on direct code review, to be still broken — see Section H. Section G adds a second thread: a nine-city search for a validated second case study, won by Lyon.

How much each fix is worth depends on C1

The pipeline produces a continuous illuminance surface and the model then reduces it to one bit per edge — above or below the agent's threshold. So a change to the physics only changes behaviour where it moves an edge across that line; an edge that stays on the same side produces identical agent decisions no matter how much its computed lux shifts.

Two consequences worth holding while reading the rest. The physics findings (A1–A3) matter less to the behavioural results than their magnitudes suggest — but the claim that the model uses modelled illuminance rather than a binary flag is correspondingly weaker, because it re-binarises immediately. Fixing C1 is what makes the rest of the precision count.

The line is also where most of the network sits. In the pipeline's actual directional output, 20.5% of entrances are dark by MIN and 46% lie in the [5, 15) band that only vulnerable agents can see as dark — so how many entrances any fix pushes across a threshold is directly measurable, and should be the yardstick for every change below.

A1 Lamps inside building footprints are permanently dark. The sight line ends at the lamp's ground coordinate, so containment means blocked from every sample point, in every direction. 4,243–25,852 lamps (4–26%) fall inside building bboxes. stradale optics and freestanding poles dominate — consistent with Turin's porticoes rather than façade mounting.

Possible fingerprint: 11.2% of directed entrances in the pipeline's own lookup read exactly 0 lux. Test against lamp containment once geometry is loaded — real geometry is now available (see the callout above) but this test has not yet been run. Occlusion logic itself confirmed unchanged 13 Sep.		 Exclude a lamp's own containing polygon from the test. Trim the line ~0.5 m at each end, or use crosses rather than intersects, so a graze stops counting as occlusion.

Resolved upstream, 16 Sep (71e06b2): a lamp within ARCADE_DEPTH_M = 5.0 m of a building footprint is treated as mounted on it (not occluded by it); deeper stays occluded. 5,873 Torino lamps sit inside a footprint; 87% within 5 m.		 high resolved
A2 Mounting height is a category error — a pole-height median is assigned to fixtures that have no pole. altezza_palo_m is 100% absent for every non-pole tipo_supporto. The 5.6 m median of non determinato reaches 21,163 non-pole lamps; DEFAULT_HEIGHT_M = 9.0 never executes. Confirmed unchanged 13 Sep, and independently recorded in bug_changelog.md's own 10 Sep audit. Group by tipo_supporto, not uso_ottica. Poles → same-street median (65% coverage, ±0.65 m), then the global pole median of 9.0 m. Non-poles → assumed by support type, with a height_source column so provenance survives downstream.

Resolved upstream, 16 Sep (71e06b2): grouped by tipo_supporto as recommended — support types with any measured height take that type's median; support types with none (no pole to measure) take a stated NO_POLE_HEIGHT_M = 4.0 m assumption instead of the pole median. Provenance tracked in altezza_source.		 high resolved
A3 The π normalisation still contradicts the falloff law — now inside a well-documented shared module, which makes the mismatch harder to spot, not easier. As of 12 Sep the physics moved into a new pipeline/lighting.py, shared by both step-2 adapters and step 3. Its docstring correctly derives Φ = πI₀ for a Lambertian hemisphere, then states the result is “the inverse-square law with the cosine correction for the surface tilt” and applies E = I·h/(h²+d²)^1.5 with I held constant across angle. That is the isotropic falloff, not the Lambertian one — a true Lambertian intensity attenuates as I₀cos θ off-axis, giving I₀h²/(h²+d²)² instead. Same ~4.6× divergence at the tail as before, just centralised and now reading as deliberate. Unchanged recommendation, more urgent to state precisely given how authoritative the new module reads: keep ÷π, switch to the consistent Lambertian falloff. The π is scale (absorbable into one coefficient, observationally equivalent to moving the 5 lux threshold); the exponent is shape, and only shape moves the spatial pattern.

Addressed upstream, 16 Sep, but not this way (71e06b2): the code owner ran mixed/lambertian/isotropic against all 44,278 Torino edges and chose isotropic (E = Ih/D³, F/2π normalisation) over the Lambertian fix this register asked for — reasoning that Lambertian's cos θ piles light under the pole and starves the mid-span, which is backwards for a cobra-head luminaire's lateral throw. Consistent normalisation+exponent either way, so the internal contradiction this finding named is gone; which of the two consistent laws is more correct for Turin's fixtures remains a real open question, not settled by this register.		 high revised upstream
A4 Occlusion ignores lamp height, and street trees are absent entirely. Building heights are complete (zero nulls), min 6.0 m, mean 15.1 m, against a mean lamp height of 8.2 m — so 2D blocking is roughly right for buildings and wrong for canopy. Add height to the sight-line test; the data supports it today. Trees need a canopy layer that doesn't exist — document as a limitation rather than approximating it. medium
A5 braccio_l_m_max is read, zero-filled and never used, so every luminaire sits over its pole rather than above the carriageway — while sample points run down the street centreline. Recorded for only 7.2% of lamps; typical arms are 1–2.5 m and point toward the road. Confirmed unchanged 13 Sep, and independently recorded in bug_changelog.md's own 10 Sep audit. Offset the lamp by the arm where it is recorded; otherwise drop the column so it stops implying the geometry is modelled. low
A6 resolved · 12 Sep   radius_m and the flat 40 m are both gone, replaced by one derived, city-wide cutoff. lighting.summation_radius_m() finds the distance at which the strongest lamp in the inventory falls below a new NEGLIGIBLE_LUX = 0.05 and uses that single radius for every KD-tree query. Addressed upstream, 16 Sep, but not this way (71e06b2): the fill is no longer silent — every row's height now records how it was set in altezza_source (measured / support-type median / no-pole assumption / global fallback), and step 3 prints the imputed share. Directional lighting B · pipeline step 4

#IssueEvidenceSuggested fixPriority
B1 The estimator is noisy, not the horizon. The 12 m window is about one lamp spacing wide, and taking MIN across it reduces the statistic to “how far is the entry node from the nearest lamp” — a property of where the junction falls in the lighting rhythm rather than of the street. This feeds the entrance-light gate. Mean nearest-neighbour spacing 11.7 m; 39% of lamps have no neighbour within 12 m and ~66% have two or fewer.

Measured on the pipeline's own directional lookup (29,062 directed edges): switching MIN → MEAN relabels 1,400 entrances (4.8%) from dark to lit, and 13.7% of two-way edges classify differently by direction — so the directional statistic earns its place.

The horizon itself is well supported: Fotios, Yang & Uttley (2015) measured pedestrian fixation on other people at 10.3 m, revised to 15 m as the recommended observation distance.

Confirmed unchanged 13 Sep: VISIBILITY_HORIZON_M = 12.0 and directionalLuxStatistic = MIN are both exactly as before.		 Keep the horizon at 12–15 m and cite Fotios — moving to 15 m would match the recommended distance exactly. Change the statistic instead: MIN → MEAN, or a distance-weighted mean. Already configurable via directionalLuxStatistic.

Statistic half resolved upstream, 15 Sep (f740c29): default switched MIN → MEAN. Horizon half (12m → 15m) is still open — VISIBILITY_HORIZON_M unchanged on main.		 medium partly resolved

GeoMason-light 2.2.0 is two different builds The server's jar was 115,882 bytes against 117,232 locally — an older build sharing the same version tag, predating a null-safety fix. distanceWeight is stale for Torino's real circuity The network-circuity fix above (self-measured, now 1.292 for Torino) exposed a second constant that doesn't track it. DestinationChoice.choose() weighs discretionary destinations by metres = straight-line × Pars.networkCircuityFactor, linearly — so only the product distanceWeight × circuity sets the choice probabilities, per the method's own docstring (“the scaling is absorbed into the coefficient”). ActivityPars.distanceWeight = 0.0012 is a static constant, fixed — per CHANGELOG.md — while circuity sat at 1.41. Circuity now self-corrects to 1.292 every run; the coefficient does not, so the product silently dropped 8.4% the moment the circuity measurement was fixed, with nobody having decided that. Re-derived by holding the product invariant: 0.0012 × (1.41 / 1.292) ≈ 0.0013096. This is a consistency fix, not a calibration — pedsim/activity/TODO.md is explicit that distanceWeight has never been fitted to real data (Audimob microdata via ISFORT is a separate, still-open request); this only stops the circuity correction being applied twice and preserves the shape the model already produced. Scoped to DestinationChoice.java alone — ActivityAgent.decideCommuteMode()/commuteMetres() and CommuteCalibration already read Pars.networkCircuityFactor live at call time and need no equivalent fix.

rejected upstream 16 Sep (1d149a4): ActivityPars.java's new Javadoc explicitly instructs against this rescaling — “compensating pins this coefficient to a circuity figure derived from a set of trips, and re-imports through the coefficient exactly the circularity that measuring circuity from the network removes.” The code owner's stated position is to leave distanceWeight = 0.0012 as an unfitted constant rather than adjust it for the circuity re-measurement, treating the drift this row flags as acceptable rather than a defect to correct.

Sources

Standards and literature the constants in section E are traced to. None of this was cited in the repository before this pass.

  • bug_changelog.md — the code owner's own defect log, March–September 2026. Its 10 September entry “Found in the lighting chain, not fixed” independently corroborates A2, A5, C1 and C2; its RNG entries document the C4 fix. Repo root, PedSimCity.
  • pedsim/night/TODO.md — written 13 September, after the first clean full-Torino run of the post-refactor code. Source for D1's fix, the C3 open question, and every item in Section F.
  • EN 13201-2:2015 — Road lighting, Part 2: Performance requirements. Defines the P classes for pedestrian areas and their maintained average illuminance: P1 = 15 · P2 = 10 · P3 = 7.5 · P4 = 5.0 · P5 = 3.0 · P6 = 2.0 lux. Anchors both the 5 lux threshold (P4) and the [5, 15] vulnerable sensitivity range (P4 → P1).
  • EN 13201-3:2015 — Road lighting, Part 3: Calculation of performance. Specifies the calculation grid; standard longitudinal point spacing is 3 m, against which the pipeline's 2 m is conservative rather than arbitrary.
  • CIE 140 — Road lighting calculations. Origin of the utilisation-factor concept and of the grid conventions EN 13201-3 adopts. Real utilisation is a curve in road width ÷ mounting height, not a per-optic constant — which is why the utilisation table is marked partial rather than sourced.
  • UNI 11248 — the Italian selection standard that assigns a lighting class to a given road, and therefore how Turin's own streets acquire their P or M class. Relevant if the modelled surface is ever compared against compliance criteria.
  • data.grandlyon.com — source for Section G's Lyon figures: points lumineux SIGERLy (lighting, EclExt national schema) and the comptage-mobilites site/channel/measure collection (pedestrian counting), both downloaded and profiled directly rather than read from metadata.
  • Géostandard EclExt (COVADIS / CNIG) — the French national data model for exterior lighting that both Lyon's and Nantes's datasets conform to, which is why both carry wattage, height and technology fields Torino's own schema lacks a full equivalent for.
  • datos.madrid.es — Madrid's runner-up figures: Unidades luminosas (farolas) and Aforos de peatones y bicicletas, both downloaded directly.
  • Fotios, S., Yang, B. & Uttley, J. (2015). Observing other pedestrians: investigating the typical distance and duration of fixation. Lighting Research & Technology. Fixation on other pedestrians measured at 10.3 m, revised to 15 m as the recommended observation distance — the anchor for the 12 m visibility horizon, and already in the manuscript bibliography via fotios2019evaluation.
  • Comparison of energy efficiency and costs related to conventional and LED road lighting installations, Energy (2022). Luminaire-level efficacy benchmarks used to check the 120 / 90 / 70 lm/W figures.

Cite the right half of EN 13201

The standard specifies luminance (cd/m²) for traffic routes and illuminance (lux) only for pedestrian areas, conflict areas and residential streets. This pipeline produces horizontal illuminance, so the P classes are the right half to invoke — and the M classes are not. Comparing modelled lux against an M-class criterion would need a road-surface reflectance assumption the model doesn't make.

Counts derived from Torino_puntiLuce.gpkg (99,742 fixtures) and Torino_buildings.gpkg (20,701 footprints), both EPSG:3003. Building-containment figures are bounding-box tests: the upper bound counts any building bbox, the lower bound only compact footprints under 1,000 m² where the bbox approximates the polygon. Spacing statistics are nearest-neighbour distances on a 1-in-50 sample, which understate along-street pole spacing.
